Brand Voice and Messaging Guidelines
How we sound, what we say, and what we never promise. 2026 edition.

This document is written for everyone who puts words in front of our members: copywriters, designers, social media managers, customer support, agencies and freelancers. It is meant to be read like a conversation rather than searched like a rulebook. If you read it once from start to finish, you should come away knowing who we are, how we talk, and where the lines are that we do not cross.

Who we are
Aurora Pantry is a subscription service that delivers small-batch, wholesome snacks and pantry staples to homes across India. The company was founded in 2016 in Pune by two friends who were tired of snack aisles full of confusing labels and promises nobody could check. Today more than 185,000 members receive a monthly Aurora box, and our catalogue has grown to 62 products made with ingredients from 140 partner farms.

Since launch we have shipped roughly 1.2 million boxes, which is a figure we are proud of but do not need to repeat in every email. When you write about our size, use the figure of 185,000 members and do not round it up. If a number you want to use is not mentioned in this document, please ask the brand team before publishing it, because a wrong number in public is much harder to fix than a missing one.

Delivery is free on orders above 499 rupees, and our support team promises a reply to every message within 24 hours. These two promises appear often in our content, so they must always be stated exactly as written here.

Our voice
Our voice is warm, honest and curious. We write the way a knowledgeable friend would talk across a kitchen counter: plain-spoken, friendly and never preachy. We are confident without being boastful, and we would rather explain something simply than impress anyone with jargon. Our writing should feel unhurried and kind, as if we have all the time in the world for the reader.

Humour is welcome when it is gentle, but we do not joke about health, money or anyone's body. We also avoid shouting. Exclamation marks are kept for genuine celebration, such as a new product launch or a member milestone, and we never use more than one in a single piece of writing. Words in capital letters are for brand names only.

Above all we want to sound like people. If a sentence would feel strange when read aloud to a friend, rewrite it. If a sentence sounds like a press release, rewrite it twice.

The words we choose
The people we serve are our members. Please call them members every time. The words user and consumer feel cold and transactional, so we never use them in anything we publish. If you catch yourself writing user, replace it with member.

We describe our food as wholesome, small-batch and nourishing, and we describe the service as a monthly pantry box. These are the phrases our members already use when they talk about us, so they are the ones we prefer. Words like farm-fresh, honest and thoughtfully sourced are also welcome, provided the sourcing team can back them up.

A few words need more care. The word cheap is out, because cheap suggests poor quality; say affordable or good value instead. The word guaranteed is also out. We cannot guarantee how anyone will feel or what results they will see, so write designed to or made to instead. We avoid the word diet when talking about our snacks and prefer balanced eating. The word healthy is too vague to mean anything on its own, so say nutritious, and only when the nutrition label supports it.

Some words are simply off limits and have no substitute. We never use detox, miracle or cure in any channel and in any context, not even jokingly or in a quotation from a member. The word superfood is not a term we use either. If you feel you need one of these words to make a sentence work, the sentence is making a promise we should not be making.

Claims and evidence
Our snacks are food, not medicine. We make no medical or health-treatment claims, and we never say or imply that our products treat, prevent or cure any condition. We never promise weight loss, an energy boost, better skin, or any other change in someone's body or health.

We avoid unsupported superlatives such as best, number one, leading or revolutionary. A superlative is only acceptable when we can point to a named independent source that the legal team has approved in writing. Any nutrition statement must match exactly what is printed on the product label. The word organic may only be used for products that carry a valid certification, and the certification should be named where space allows.

We do not compare ourselves with named competitors. Testimonials must come from real members who have given written permission, and we never present a single member's experience as typical. If a claim would make a reasonable reader raise an eyebrow, it needs evidence before it goes anywhere near a headline.

What we say about ourselves
Our tagline is “Good food, honestly made.” It appears on our packaging, on the homepage and in every email signature, and it should always be written exactly like that, with the comma and the full stop. For onboarding messages and welcome emails we like to use the line “Snacking you can feel good about.” When a short description of the company is needed, for example in a press note or a partner listing, use our standard boilerplate: Aurora Pantry delivers small-batch, wholesome snacks from farms we know by name.

Two other messages come up again and again and are always welcome: that our snacks are made in small batches, and that every box tells you which farms its ingredients came from. These are things we can prove, which is exactly why we like saying them.

Writing for each channel
In email, we keep subject lines short, ideally under 50 characters, and we never write them in capital letters. The first line of a subject should say something real about what is inside. We use at most one exclamation mark in an email, and we sign off warmly with the tagline.

On social media the tone relaxes a little. Posts should stay under 280 characters, use no more than 3 hashtags, and use emojis sparingly, one or two at most. A joke is fine; a pun about a product is fine; a joke at a member's expense never is. Replies to members should always be personal, never copy and paste.

In paid advertising we are at our most careful. Every advertisement that mentions a discount or an offer must carry the line “Terms apply.” We avoid pressure phrases such as last chance or buy now before it is gone, and we never use a superlative in an ad. Ads follow all the claims rules above, with no exceptions for the sake of space.

On the blog and in longer articles we have room to explain. We can be more detailed, tell stories about the farms, and answer questions. Any factual or nutritional statement in an article should be linked to its source. Headlines should describe what the article contains and never promise a result.

Looking the part
Our logo is a rising sun over a small leaf, and it always needs clear space around it equal to the height of the leaf. Our two main colours are a deep forest green and a warm sunrise amber, with off-white as the background. We photograph food in natural daylight, on real tables, and we prefer crumbs and imperfect hands to polished studio shots. Our headings are set in a friendly rounded typeface, and body text is always set in a simple sans serif font for easy reading. These visual details matter, but they matter less than the words, which is why most of this document is about them.

Our official social media handle is @aurorapantry on every platform. Please do not create look-alike accounts or campaign handles without asking.
